## 5.12 Governance

Authorized users shall be able to manage nonprofit governance records:

- **Board members**: linked to `people`, with role/title, term start/end, and active status.
- **Meetings**: date, type (board, committee, annual, other), facilitator and notes-taker (both `people`), attendees, and associated:
  - **Agendas**: for board meetings, built from a versioned, seeded agenda template (issue #166) covering the standing "Ongoing Board Items" review sections (Finance & Fundraising, Legal & Nonprofit, Events, Community & Partnerships, Marketing & Social, Operations, Technology & Website — each with fixed discussion topics), new business, upcoming dates, a parking lot, and next-meeting info, alongside the meeting's action items and decisions/votes. A section either holds **per-meeting updates/decisions-needed text** or, since issue #1240, **names a data source** — the Events module, or the community calendar filtered by category and item type — in which case it shows those records and asks for one **Discussion** box instead of the pair, because a board should not be retyping what another module already holds. Template version 2 of `board_meeting` sources Events, Community & Partnerships and Marketing & Social; version 1 sources nothing, so an agenda pinned to it keeps the pair on all seven sections.
  - **Simple agendas** (issue #1623): only `board` meetings get the structured agenda above. A `committee`, `annual` or `other` meeting gets an ordered list of items, each a title and optional notes, edited with the same view/edit toggle (add, rename, reorder, remove) and with no template picker or module feeds; its minutes follow the items, one entry per item. The shape is fixed when the agenda is first saved: if the meeting's type later changes to the other side of board/non-board, the agenda keeps rendering in the shape it was written in, read-only, with a short note — it is never converted, and minutes already started are never touched. Non-board meetings that already held a board agenda before #1623 render it the same way.
  - **Committee agendas pre-filled from the linked event** (issue #1624): when a `committee` meeting is linked to an event and has no agenda yet, opening its agenda creates one from that event's open work — the phase tasks the dashboard's Outstanding tasks run on (planning, attendance, after-report, retro), its open checklist items, and its open retro action items plus those carried forward from earlier events in the same program (#1578), one item each. Each item freezes a reference to the event its work sits on (`source: {key, kind, id, label, date}`), shown as a link on the agenda and frozen into the minutes snapshot as the item's `planned.references`. Pre-filled items are ordinary items afterwards. The pre-fill runs once: once a row exists, even an emptied one, it is never filled again. **Refresh from event** appends only work not on the agenda yet, deduplicated by `source.key`, and never removes or rewords an item. Board meetings pull events through their `events` feed instead; annual and other meetings start empty. If the event has no open work the agenda stays empty, with a short note.
  - **Minutes**: what happened, recorded against the agenda rather than instead of it. Starting minutes freezes the agenda into an ordered item list (opening checklist, carried-over action items, the standing sections, decisions, new business, upcoming dates, parking lot, next meeting); the notetaker writes per-item notes keyed to that frozen list, plus general closing notes. Minutes are a draft until the notetaker finalizes them, and finalized minutes are read-only until reopened — separately from the board's approval of them, which happens at the _next_ meeting (issue #1199). The Minutes tab on a meeting is where they are written (issue #1200): it has **no Save button** — notes save themselves about a second after typing stops, and again when a field is left or the tab is switched away from, with a quiet inline status line as the receipt. A failed save keeps the text and retries with backoff, and the browser still warns before a refresh while anything is unsaved. An action item raised while a section is being discussed is captured from that section's card and linked back to it through `governance_meeting_action_items.minutes_item_key`, so recording one never means navigating away mid-meeting — the complaint the whole set exists for. A **quick-reference panel** beside the notes carries the rest of what a notetaker used to leave for (issue #1201): who is present (with the attendance toggle), open action items carried over from earlier meetings, this meeting's own open items, its decisions, and what the last meeting decided — a sticky column at a desk, a sheet behind a "Reference" button on a phone. An action item can be raised from the panel too, which records it against the meeting rather than against one section. Minutes **export** the way agendas do — print or copy as Markdown or plain text, with action items grouped under the item they were raised under and unlinked ones under a heading of their own; there is no share link, because governance content is never public (the decision record for issue #524). Notes are written in a plain textarea and read back **rendered as Markdown** — the bullets and bold a notetaker types are shown as bullets and bold on the finalized minutes, in the export's print view and in the approval dialog, though never in the editor itself.

    The agenda's own free-text field is called **"Agenda notes"** since issue #1201 (it was "Meeting notes" while it _was_ the minutes): it is pre-meeting context, and what happened belongs in the Minutes tab.

  - **Action items** and **decisions**: list-based, per meeting; decisions carry an optional topic and vote result alongside the discussion description, so a decision can double as a lightweight "Decisions & Votes" agenda entry.
  - **Resolutions**: motion text, mover/seconder, vote outcome, and effective date
- **Bylaws**: the governing document, with effective date and amendment history.
- **Policies**: named policies (e.g. whistleblower, document retention, conflict of interest policy itself), each with a category and effective date.
- **Conflict of interest**: per-person annual disclosure statements, on-file date, and any noted conflicts.
- **Annual requirements**: recurring compliance items (e.g. annual report, IRS Form 990, state charitable registration renewal) with due date, completion status/date, and responsible party.
- **Nonprofit status tracking**: a phased checklist tracking progress toward 501(c)(3) formation, grouped by the roadmap's Gate/Phase labels, with each item carrying a status (not started / in progress / done), an optional owner (`people`), and an optional due date. Entries are updated manually by admin/board — these are real-world legal filings with no transactional trigger elsewhere in the portal. Not a weighted percent-complete meter; a derived "N of M complete" count is shown per phase and overall instead. Portal-only, gated on the same `governance` resource as other governance records (issues #145/#146).
- **Open work** (issue #1658): one list, `/portal/governance/open-work`, of the work the module tracks: open meeting action items, nonprofit-status milestones and annual requirements that aren't done, `planned` grants (by `application_deadline`) and partnerships with a `next_step_date`. It shows each item's type, owner, date-only due date, status and a link to its record or meeting, filters by owner (anyone, **Mine** through the signed-in user's `people` row, unassigned, or one person), type, due (overdue, due this week) and status (open by default), and sorts overdue first. Inline edits go through each source's existing Server Action and form parser, never a new write path: status, owner and due date for action items, milestones and annual requirements; the existing edit modal for grants and partnerships. The row type, filter vocabulary and table (`src/lib/portal/task-list.ts`, `src/components/portal/task-table.tsx`) are shared with the Events task list (#1661), and the union query (`src/lib/portal/governance-open-work.ts`) is reusable by a later org-wide My tasks page.
- **Owner and due filters on the list pages** (issue #1659): Meetings has an **Action items** tab (`?tab=action-items`) beside the meetings table — Open work's query restricted to action items, with its owner, due and status filters and its inline actions. The Nonprofit status and Annual requirements checklists take the same owner and due filters (no status filter: each checklist shows every status, and its "N of M complete" counts stay the whole checklist's). Every filter is held in the URL, so a filtered view can be linked to.

The content of an individual governance record (a policy's text, a set of minutes, a signed bylaws amendment, etc.) is not required to take one fixed form. A record may hold a document, a free-text body, or both, so staff can start with a quick note and attach the scanned/signed file once it exists. The document is **either** an external link (e.g. to a file in the organization's Google Drive/OneDrive) **or** one uploaded file (a PDF or a photo of the page, up to 10 MB) in the private `documents` Storage bucket (issue #1489): never both, and there is no multi-file attachment list (see [§2](../technical-spec.md#2-goals-and-non-goals)). An uploaded file is never public — the portal signs a URL for it server-side, on the reader's own session, and the bucket's policies let only holders of `governance: view` in the same tenant resolve one, so a board member reaches a document only through the portal. Each record's view shows a preview: a thumbnail for an image, the file name with Open for a PDF, the host with Open for a link. `resolutions`, `bylaws`, `policies`, `conflict_of_interest_disclosures`, and `annual_requirements` are implemented this way (`external_link`/`document_path`/`body_text`); `meeting_minutes` keeps a link to the agenda's `external_link` in its snapshot (not its `document_path`, which stays on the agenda) and holds its substance in structured per-item notes rather than one free-text body (issue #1199); `agendas` keeps the same `external_link`/`document_path` pair but replaced its single free-text body with the structured, template-driven columns described above (issue #166), pinned to the template version an agenda was built from so later template revisions don't retroactively change a saved agenda. A general `file_attachments` table is not planned — see [§2](../technical-spec.md#2-goals-and-non-goals) and [§6](../technical-spec.md#6-proposed-data-model).

Governance records contain sensitive organizational and personal information and must not be public. Access is limited to the `admin` and `board` roles — see the entitlement matrix in [§5.3](access-control.md#53-authentication-and-authorization).

Governance (board members, meetings/agendas/minutes/action items/decisions, resolutions, bylaws, policies, conflict-of-interest disclosures, annual requirements, nonprofit-status tracking, Open work) is fully implemented, not a placeholder.

## 6. Data model — Governance

Meetings, agendas, minutes, action items, decisions, board members, nonprofit-status tracking, bylaws, policies, conflict-of-interest disclosures, and annual requirements are all implemented.

- `board_members`: links a `people` record with role/title, term start/end, and active status. `bylaws.md` Article VI's Board/Advisory Committees concept (issue #12) is deliberately out of scope for this data model for now — no `committees` entity exists, and committee membership is not tracked here.
- `governance_meetings`: date, type (board, committee, annual, other), status, `facilitator_person_id`/`notetaker_person_id` (both → `people`, issue #166), optional `event_id` (→ `events`, issue #1622; any meeting type, same-tenant by the composite key, cleared when the event is deleted — the meeting shows it in its details card and the event lists its meetings on Overview); associated `governance_meeting_attendees` link table to `people`
- `agendas`: linked to a `governance_meetings` row (unique). `external_link` or `document_path` (#1489, below) plus a structured, template-driven body (issue #166): `template_id`/`template_version_id` (pinned at save time, → `agenda_templates`/`agenda_template_versions`), `ongoing_items` (jsonb, keyed by template section, holding `{updates?, decisions_needed?, discussion?}` — a manual section writes the first two, a sourced one the third, and reading tolerates all three on any section so revising a template never hides text somebody typed, #1240), `new_business`/`parking_lot` (jsonb string arrays), `upcoming_dates` (jsonb array of date/description/owner), `next_meeting_date`/`next_meeting_topics`, and `body_text` (repurposed as free-form meeting notes). `format` (`board` | `simple`, default `board`, issue #1623) records which shape the row was written in, and `items` (jsonb array, `[{id, title, notes?, source?}]`, `source` since #1624) holds a simple agenda's ordered items; ids are stable (a UUID, kept to the alphabet minutes note keys allow) so a rename or reorder keeps its minutes note attached. The server picks the format from the meeting's type and refuses to write over a row in the other format.
- `agenda_templates` / `agenda_template_versions`: a small versioned catalog (issue #166), modelled on the content brief templates that #1230 later retired — a template's `current_version_id` points at its live `sections` (each `{key, label, topics, source?}`, one per standing "Ongoing Board Items" subsection); revising a template inserts a new version rather than mutating one an existing agenda is pinned to. Seeded with a single `board_meeting` template covering the seven standard sections. A section's optional `source` is `{kind: "events"}` or `{kind: "calendar", categories?, item_types?}` (#1240); `categories` names `calendar_categories.key` values, not labels, so a tenant renaming a category changes nothing and a deactivated key is skipped rather than raised. An unrecognised `kind` renders the section as manual — a version is immutable, so one naming a kind this deployment does not ship is a real state, and a blank section would be worse than the text boxes. Both tables are per tenant, so version 2 was inserted per tenant by `20260919010000_agenda_template_section_sources.sql`, which leaves version 1 rows untouched and skips any tenant already revised past version 1.
- `meeting_minutes`: **implemented** (issue #1199) — one row per `governance_meetings` row (unique per tenant, cascading). `agenda_snapshot` (jsonb) is the agenda frozen at the moment minutes were started: `{version, meeting_date, template_id, template_version_id, external_link, items[]}`, where each item is `{key, label, kind, planned?}` and `kind` is one of `opening`, `carried_over`, `section`, `decisions`, `new_business`, `upcoming_dates`, `parking_lot`, `next_meeting`, or `item` — a simple agenda's (issue #1623, snapshot version 3), which freezes as the opening, one `item:<agenda item id>` entry per item, then decisions. That one ordered array is simultaneously the note keys, the render order and the export order. It is a copy rather than a join so that editing the agenda mid-meeting cannot move the structure under the notetaker; keys (`section:finance_fundraising`, `new_business:0`) are assigned once at seed time and never recomputed, which is what makes the positional ones safe. `notes` (jsonb) is a flat `{item key: text}` map merged with `||` by `save_meeting_minutes_draft()` — **last-write-wins per note key**, so two people typing in the same section still overwrite each other; there is no locking and no Realtime, by design. `body_text` holds general/closing notes, seeded from `agendas.body_text` when the minutes are started. Lifecycle is `status` (`draft`/`final`) plus `finalized_at`/`finalized_by`, enforced by a check constraint, with content edits to a final row refused by a `before update` trigger rather than by a policy predicate — a `using (status = 'draft')` policy would also block reopening, since the row is still `final` at that moment. `approved_at`/`approved_by`/`approved_at_meeting_id` mirror the board's approval, which is a different event from finalization and is also recorded on `governance_meetings.minutes_approved_at/by` at the approving meeting; since issue #1201 the "Approve previous meeting minutes" step writes both, reads the prior meeting's `meeting_minutes` row (falling back to its agenda notes, and saying so, for meetings older than this table), and **refuses while that row is still a draft** — the board should not assent to text the notetaker is still writing. `governance_meeting_action_items.minutes_item_key` (nullable, no FK) links an action raised during a section back to that section's snapshot item.

  **Why this table exists after #408 dropped `minutes`.** The dropped table held `external_link` + `body_text` and nothing else — a genuine duplicate of the agenda's two fields, and removing it was right. This is a different thing: a frozen ordered snapshot of the agenda, per-item notes keyed to it, and a draft → final lifecycle with a finalizer. None of the three has anywhere to live on `agendas`, because the agenda is the plan and stays editable. It is not a second free-text box.

- `governance_meeting_action_items`: linked to a `governance_meetings` row, description, owner (`people`), due date, status (open/done)
- `governance_meeting_decisions`: linked to a `governance_meetings` row, description (the discussion), decision date, and optional `topic`/`vote_result` (issue #166) so a decision can serve as an agenda's "Decisions & Votes" entry — distinct from `resolutions` below, which are formal motions
- `resolutions`: linked to a `governance_meetings` row (optional), motion text, mover/seconder (`people`), vote outcome, effective date
- `bylaws`: **implemented** (issue #38) — `version`, `effective_date`, `amendment_summary`. Each amendment is its own row rather than mutating a shared "current" record, per the app's records-with-history philosophy ([§2](../technical-spec.md#2-goals-and-non-goals) goal 4); the row with the latest `effective_date` is the current bylaws, and the full row list is the amendment history — no separate history table. `/portal/governance/bylaws` shows the current version plus a history table of prior versions.
- `policies`: **implemented** (issue #38) — `name`, `category` (free text — spec gives examples, no fixed taxonomy), `effective_date`, `version`. Same one-row-per-revision approach as `bylaws`. `/portal/governance/policies` is a searchable/filterable list.
- `conflict_of_interest_disclosures`: **implemented** (issue #39) — linked to a `people` record, `disclosure_year`, `on_file_date`, `notes`, unique per person/year.
- `annual_requirements`: **implemented** (issue #39) — `name`, `due_date`, `status` (not_started/in_progress/done), `completed_at` (derived from status transitions in app logic), responsible `people` record.
- `nonprofit_status_milestones`: **implemented** (issues #145/#146, #356) — `description`, `phase`, optional `owner_person_id` (→ `people`), `due_date`, `status` (not_started/in_progress/done/cancelled), `sort_order` (stable row order within a phase, since seeded rows share one `created_at`), optional free-text `notes`. A plain checklist (no percent-complete meter, by design), seeded from `planning/governance/NONPROFIT_FORMATION.md`'s Phase 1–5 checklist, gated by the existing `governance` resource (no new resource needed) at `/portal/governance/nonprofit-status`.

`resolutions`, `bylaws`, `policies`, `conflict_of_interest_disclosures`, and `annual_requirements` each hold their substantive content via nullable `external_link`, `document_path` and `body_text` columns, per §5.12. `document_path` (#1489) is an object path in the private `documents` bucket, `{tenant_id}/governance/{uuid}/{file name}`; a `*_one_document` check allows at most one of `external_link` and `document_path`, and a `*_document_path_in_tenant` check pins the path under the row's own tenant and the `governance/` folder. `agendas` carries the same pair and the same checks. Objects no row references are swept after a day by the daily orphan purge (`src/lib/storage/documents-purge.ts`). `agendas` moved to the structured, template-driven column set described above, and `meeting_minutes` (issue #1199) to a frozen item list with per-item notes. There is no `file_attachment_id` column and none is planned — a general `file_attachments` table is not being built (see [§2](../technical-spec.md#2-goals-and-non-goals)).
